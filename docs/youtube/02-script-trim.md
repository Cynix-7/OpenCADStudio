## 00:06–00:25 WHAT TRIM DOES

Screen: before/after of a classic trim: two crossing lines, one gets cut at
the intersection.

overlap, or a grid of lines you need to clean up, TRIM is the tool. Let's
build a quick example."

[Screen: draw a rectangle, then a line through it]

## 00:25–01:20 STEP BY STEP

VO: "Here's a rectangle with a line through it. I want to cut the part of the
line inside the rectangle away. Type TRIM — or press TR, Enter — at the
command line."

[Screen: type TRIM, show command-line prompt]

VO: "First, pick your cutting edges — the objects that do the cutting. I'll
select the rectangle's top and bottom lines, then press Enter."

[Screen: select the two rectangle edges, Enter]

VO: "Now click the part of the line inside the rectangle. Watch it disappear.
Press Escape, and the line is cut cleanly at both edges."

## 01:20–01:50 POWER TIP — TRIM EVERYTHING

Screen: new messy drawing; type TRIM, immediately press Enter without
selecting; click lines.

VO: "Power tip: you don't even have to pick cutting edges. Start TRIM and just
press Enter — everything in the drawing becomes a cutting edge. Then click
whatever you want to trim away. For cleaning up a busy drawing, this is the
fastest way."

## 01:50–02:30 TRIM WITH FILLETS' BEST FRIEND (quick combo)

Screen: two lines meeting at a corner, one overshoots; TRIM the overshoot;
then FILLET the corner.

VO: "Here's the classic combo: two lines that should meet at a corner. Trim
the overshoot, then round the corner with FILLET — radius 10 — and you have a
clean, machined-looking corner. I'll cover FILLET in its own video; the link
is on screen."
