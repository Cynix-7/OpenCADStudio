## 00:06–00:30 WHAT HATCH DOES

Screen: examples — crosshatch (sections), diagonal (materials), solid fill,
dots (concrete/insulation).

VO: "HATCH fills a closed area with a pattern: crosshatch for cut sections,
diagonals for materials, solid fills for emphasis, dots for insulation or
concrete. It's what makes technical drawings readable."

## 00:30–01:20 STEP BY STEP

Screen: a wall outline (two rectangles offset, closed); type HATCH.

VO: "Here's a wall section — two outlines. Type HATCH — or H, Enter. Click
inside the area between the outlines — the app finds the closed boundary
itself — and the default pattern fills it. That's the whole core workflow:
pick a closed area, get a fill."

[Screen: click inside; pattern appears]

## 01:20–02:00 PATTERNS & SCALE

Screen: open the pattern list; switch to a different pattern; change scale.

VO: "The pattern list has the classic set — ANSI crosshatch, diagonal,
brick, dots, and more. Pick one and it updates instantly. Scale matters: too
small and it looks like a solid block, too big and it reads as lines. Bump the
scale up or down until it looks right for your drawing size."

[Screen: pattern switch + scale changes]

## 02:00–02:40 EDITING A HATCH

Screen: click the hatch → properties; change pattern/scale/angle; then
HATCHEDIT on an existing hatch.

VO: "Hatches are objects, not stickers — click one and the Properties panel
lets you change pattern, scale, and angle anytime. Or double-click it, and
HATCHEDIT opens the same options. If the geometry changes — stretch the wall —
the hatch follows, because it's tied to the boundary."

## 02:40–03:05 HATCH ON ITS OWN LAYER

Screen: create HATCH layer, move hatch to it, freeze it to show the trick.

VO: "Pro habit: put hatches on their own layer. In a floor plan, freeze the
HATCH layer to see just the walls, or print a version without fills. It keeps
the drawing flexible."
