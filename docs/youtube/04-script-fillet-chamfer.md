## 00:06–00:30 WHAT THEY DO

Screen: side-by-side labels — FILLET = rounded arc; CHAMFER = flat bevel.

VO: "FILLET rounds a corner with an arc. CHAMFER cuts it with a straight line.
Two corners that look almost the same — very different in manufacturing, where
rounded corners relieve stress and bevels make edges easier to weld or
deburr."

## 00:30–01:15 FILLET STEP BY STEP

Screen: two perpendicular lines meeting at a corner.

VO: "Here are two lines meeting at a corner. Type FILLET — or F, Enter. The
prompt shows the current radius; it defaults to zero, so type R for radius,
then 10, Enter."

[Screen: type FILLET, R, 10, Enter]

VO: "Now click the first line, then the second. The corner becomes a smooth
10-unit arc. Change the radius and do it again — different curve, same two
clicks."

[Screen: fillet at radius 20 to show the difference]

## 01:15–01:50 FILLET TIP — ZERO RADIUS = CLEAN CORNER

Screen: two lines that don't quite meet / overshoot; FILLET with R=0 snaps
them into a perfect corner.

VO: "Pro tip: FILLET with radius zero is a cleanup tool. These two lines don't
quite meet. FILLET, R, 0, pick both — they snap into a perfect sharp corner,
with the overshoot trimmed automatically. It's the fastest way to fix sloppy
corners."

## 01:50–02:25 CHAMFER STEP BY STEP

Screen: same two lines; type CHAMFER.

VO: "Now CHAMFER. Type CHAMFER — or CHA. It asks for two distances; keep them
equal, say 10 and 10, then pick the two lines. The corner is now a flat bevel.
For a 45-degree look, equal distances are exactly that."

## 02:25–02:50 WHEN TO USE WHICH

Screen: graphic — machine part with fillets (stress relief) vs beveled edge.

VO: "Quick rule: fillet for smooth curves, stress relief, and anything that
needs to look machined. Chamfer for weld prep, deburring, and chamfered
holes. Both update live — undo with Ctrl+Z and try the other."
